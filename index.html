📄 Understanding Epilepsy

Information, guidance and care advice

🧠 What is Epilepsy?

Epilepsy is a long-term brain condition that causes repeated seizures.

🌍About 50 million people worldwide have epilepsy.

Symptoms

Seizure symptoms vary depending on the type. Symptoms may include:

  • 🌀
    Short-term confusion
  • 👀
    A staring spell
  • 💪
    Stiff muscles
  • 🦵
    Jerking movements of the arms and legs
  • 😵
    Loss of consciousness

🔍 Causes Overview

Epilepsy has no identifiable cause in about half the people. In the other half:

  • 🧬Genetic influence: Some types of epilepsy run in families.
  • 🤕Head trauma: Car accidents or traumatic injuries can cause epilepsy.
  • 🧠Brain factors: Brain tumours or abnormal blood vessels.
  • 🦠Infections: Meningitis, HIV, viral encephalitis.
  • 🧩Developmental conditions: People with autism are more likely to have epilepsy.

⚡ Seizure Triggers

Triggers don't cause epilepsy but may trigger seizures in people who have it. Common triggers:

  • 💊
    Missed medication doses
  • 😴
    Lack of sleep or extreme tiredness
  • 😰
    Stress or anxiety
  • 🍷
    Alcohol or recreational drugs
  • 💡
    Flashing or flickering lights
  • 🤒
    Illness or fever
  • 🍽️
    Skipping meals or low blood sugar

💊 Treatment

Treatment can help reduce or stop seizures. Options include:

  • 💊Anti-epileptic drugs (AED) — taken daily to control seizures
  • 🏥Surgery — considered when medication does not control seizures
  • ⚡Vagus nerve stimulation — to reduce seizure frequency
  • 🥑Dietary therapy — such as the ketogenic diet

Always follow your neurologist's advice for your specific treatment.

✅ Do's and Don'ts for Epilepsy Patients

✅ Do's

  • Use and carry a pill caddy so medications are always available.
  • Always keep a stock of medicine so you never run out.
  • Get enough exercise and rest.
  • Know your seizure triggers and avoid them.
  • Use relaxation techniques like yoga and meditation.
  • Inform those around you about your condition.

❌ Don'ts

  • Do not be embarrassed about taking your medication.
  • Do not try medicines recommended by friends without consulting your doctor.
  • Do not consume alcohol or recreational drugs.
  • Do not take other medications without your doctor's approval.
  • Do not feel pressured to join activities that may trigger a seizure.
  • Do not ignore high-risk situations — ensure someone is with you.

👁️ Do's and Don'ts for Witnessing a Seizure

EPILEPSY

✅ Do's

  • Stay calm and stay with the person.
  • Time how long the seizure lasts.
  • Keep the person safe — clear away sharp objects.
  • Gently cushion their head.
  • After convulsions stop, turn them onto their side.
  • Call emergency services if the seizure lasts more than 5 minutes.
  • Stay with them until they are fully alert.

❌ Don'ts

  • Do not restrain them.
  • Do not put anything in their mouth.
  • Do not give food or water until fully alert.
  • Do not leave them alone.
  • Do not move them unless in immediate danger.
  • Do not panic — stay calm.
