📄 Understanding Epilepsy

Information, guidance and care advice

🧠 Overview

Epilepsy is a chronic neurological spectrum disorder characterized by recurrent, unprovoked seizures caused by sudden surges of abnormal electrical activity in the brain.1,2

A single seizure does not mean a person has epilepsy. A formal diagnosis generally requires at least two unprovoked seizures occurring more than 24 hours apart.

⚠️ Symptoms

Seizure symptoms vary depending on the type of seizure. Because epilepsy is caused by certain activity in the brain, seizures can affect any brain process. Seizure symptoms may include:1

  • Short-term confusion
  • A staring spell
  • Loss of consciousness
  • Unusual sensations
  • Jerking movements of the arms and legs
  • Unusual smells, tastes or feelings
  • Difficulty in speaking or responding during an episode

🔍 Causes and Diagnosis

  • 🧬Causes: Often unknown; known causes include brain injury, stroke, tumors, genetic/developmental conditions, and brain infections.1,2
  • 🩺Diagnosis: Generally ≥2 unprovoked seizures >24 hours apart, or 1 seizure with a high risk of recurrence.1,2
  • 🔬Diagnostic Tools: Neurological examination, blood tests, EEG, and MRI help identify seizure activity and possible structural causes.

MRI — Magnetic Resonance Imaging

⚡ Types of Seizures

Seizures do not all look the same; they depend entirely on which part of the brain the electrical disruption occurs in. The CDC divides them into two primary categories:1,2

  • 🎯1. Focal Seizures: Begin in one area of the brain and may occur with preserved awareness or impaired awareness.
  • 🌐2. Generalized Seizures: Involve both sides of the brain from the start. Major types include absence seizures and tonic-clonic seizures.

💊 Treatment Options

While epilepsy cannot be cured, it can be successfully managed.

  • 💊Anti-Seizure Medications (ASMs): First-line treatment; approximately 70% of people may achieve good seizure control with appropriate medication.1,2
  • ⚡Surgery & Neurostimulation: For drug-resistant epilepsy, surgery or neurostimulation therapies such as VNS, RNS, and DBS may help reduce seizure frequency.1,2
  • 🥑Dietary Therapy: A medically supervised ketogenic diet may help reduce seizures, particularly in some children with difficult-to-control epilepsy.1,2

🛡️ Managing Epilepsy1,2

Managing epilepsy effectively involves a combination of medical treatment, lifestyle habits, and clear safety planning.1,2

  • 💊
    Take medication exactly as prescribed
  • 🔍
    Explore other treatments if needed
  • 🚫
    Never stop medications abruptly

🚑 Safety & First Aid Prep

  • 📝Create a Seizure Action Plan: Work with your doctor to build a written emergency roadmap. Share this plan with your family, friends, coworkers, or school staff so they know what to do.1,2
  • 🏠
    Practice household and water safety:
    • 🚿Take showers instead of baths to prevent accidental drowning.
    • 🦺Use the "buddy system" and wear a life jacket when swimming or near water.
    • 🔥Cook on back burners, pad sharp corners, and avoid open fires when alone.1

🚨When to contact emergency services: Seek emergency medical help immediately if the seizure lasts longer than 5 minutes, if they have multiple seizures back-to-back, if they are injured, or if they do not wake up after the shaking stops.1,2
